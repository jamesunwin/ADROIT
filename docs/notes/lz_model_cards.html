Research note · direct detection · 11 October 2026

The conventions behind LZ's limits on the twenty covariant WIMP–nucleon interactions

Prepared with ADROIT, an agentic dark-matter research system, which supplied the recomputations
Abstract

LZ's 2026 extended-window search reports 90% CL limits on a dimensionless coupling d for the twenty covariant interactions L1–L20 of Anand, Fitzpatrick and Haxton. An independent recomputation reproduces thirteen of them within the band set by experiment-side approximations, but differs for seven by factors between 3 and 104. Six of the seven are conventions, not physics. LZ normalises momenta by mM = mN, and its limits for L5, L7, L8, L13 and L17 correspond to the interactions written with the vector currents χ̄γμχ and N̄γμN in place of (Pμ/mM) χ̄χ and (Kμ/mM) N̄N, the substitution LZ's 2024 paper made explicitly for L6 and L9. The Gordon identity predicts the resulting factors, mN/2mχ and ½, and for L7 an extra O9 term that dominates below 50 GeV. A pair test that cancels every experiment-side effect confirms the factors to 3–5%. L6 differs by the relative sign of its O1 and O3 terms. O3 is its only term odd in the relative velocity v⃗⊥, so a v⃗⊥ taken the other way round reproduces LZ, while no convention for q can. LZ's local significances, which test the spectral shapes, confirm the L6, L7 and L13 forms. With these conventions every card but one lands within 0.64–1.37 of LZ's limits, the spread of the cards that never had a convention difference. The exception, L12, rests on a near-exact cancellation between its O12 and O15 terms in xenon. LZ's limits correspond to that cancellation spoiled at the 5% level, which makes them 6–15 times stronger (isoscalar) than an exact calculation gives.

Why this matters

An experiment's limit on d is useful to a model builder only if the meaning of d is known: which Lagrangian, which momentum scale, which sign conventions. The significances and look-elsewhere effect LZ reports are unaffected by any constant rescaling of d at fixed mass. Absolute couplings are affected. For example, reading LZ's L5 limit at mχ = 1 TeV as a limit on Anand et al.'s L5 overstates the allowed coupling by a factor of about 4300 (with mM = mN). This note identifies the conventions LZ's numbers follow, states how to convert them, and flags the one case, L12, where the numbers depend on a delicate cancellation.

Conventions

Anand, Fitzpatrick and Haxton [3] build twenty covariant interactions Lj from the bilinears χ̄χ, χ̄γ5χ, χ̄Pμχ, χ̄Pμγ5χ, χ̄iσμνqνχ and χ̄γμγ5χ and their nucleon counterparts, with P = p + p′, K = k + k′ and q = p′ − p. Every momentum appears as P/mM, K/mM or q/mM; their Table 1 gives the nonrelativistic coefficients ci of the operators Oi for unit-normalised spinors, and their Eq. (68) puts the coupling in units of 1/mM2. Their script's default is mM = mv = 246.2 GeV.

LZ's 2026 paper [1] cites that table and fixes the unit coupling to 1/mv2, with recoil spectra from WimPyDD [5]. Its 2024 covariant analysis [2] states two further choices that the 2026 numbers turn out to follow. It sets mM = mN, and its Table I writes L6 and L9 with a vector current γμ where Anand et al. have Pμ/mM or Kμ/mM, and L12 and L18 as dipole–dipole interactions. Throughout, mM = mN and the coupling unit 1/mv2 are used for both codes, so the comparisons below isolate the remaining conventions.

Method

The recomputation

ADROIT's LZ module [7] recomputes each of LZ's 515 elastic table entries (20 interactions, isoscalar and isovector, 13 masses from 10 GeV to 4 TeV) from the exposure (220 d × 4.71 t), the published efficiency, the recoil spectrum computed with the revised xenon responses of Haxton and McElvain [6] and the recommended halo [9], and a one-event likelihood around LZ's 248 keV event of interest. That likelihood is an approximation: it leaves out electron-recoil leakage below about 110 keV. Its accuracy is set by the cards whose conventions are not in question. For them dADROIT/dLZ runs from about 1.3 at 10 GeV to 0.8–1.0 above 100 GeV, the same trend for every interaction. The agreement band used below is ADROIT's pre-registered validation tolerance, a factor 1.3 in rate, so 0.877–1.140 in d.

A test free of the experiment

LZ notes [1, supplement] that five pairs of interactions have identical recoil spectra up to a constant: L1/L5, L2/L8, L3/L17, L4/L20 and L11/L14. Each pair reduces to a single operator. For such a pair the ratio of dADROIT/dLZ between the two partners cancels the efficiency, the halo, the statistics and the nuclear response exactly, and leaves only the ratio of the two codes' coefficients. This measures LZ's relative normalisation directly.

Hypothesis tests

For the other cards, alternative coefficient sets are recomputed with the same module and compared with LZ mass by mass. A hypothesis is accepted when it brings a card into the same band, with the same mass trend, as the unaffected cards.

The Gordon identity explains the factors

For on-shell spinors the vector current splits into a convective and a magnetic part:

ū(p′) γμ u(p) = ū(p′) [ Pμ + iσμνqν ] u(p) / 2m(1)

So (Pμ/mM) χ̄χ ≈ (2mχ/mM) χ̄γμχ and (Kμ/mM) N̄N ≈ (2mN/mM) N̄γμN, up to the magnetic terms. Writing an interaction with χ̄γμχ in place of (Pμ/mM) χ̄χ therefore multiplies its leading coefficient by mM/2mχ = mN/2mχ. Writing N̄γμN in place of (Kμ/mM) N̄N multiplies it by mM/2mN = ½. Next to the pseudoscalar bilinears χ̄γ5χ and N̄γ5N there is no such vector current to substitute, and indeed every card in which P and K appear only there (L16, L18–L20) agrees as transcribed.

L6: the direction of v⃗⊥

LZ's 2024 Table I gives L6 = χ̄γμχ N̄ iσμα(qα/mM) N four terms: O1 and O4 with q⃗2, and O3 and O6. An explicit-spinor reduction of that Lagrangian gives the same four terms with the same signs. Computed that way, the limits are 2–8 times stronger than LZ's. Reversing the relative sign of the O1 and O3 terms brings both isospins into the band at every mass. Reversing O4 and O6 changes nothing.

The sign convention for q cannot do this. The Lagrangian is linear in qα, so taking the nucleon's momentum transfer in place of the dark matter's multiplies every term by −1, an overall sign that no rate sees. The direction of the relative velocity can. O3 = i S⃗N·(q⃗/mN × v⃗⊥) is the only term here odd in v⃗⊥, and it interferes with O1 through xenon's Φ″–M response. A code that takes v⃗⊥ the other way round flips every operator linear in it (O3, O5, O7, O8, O12–O15). Among the twenty interactions only L6 has such a term with a strong interference partner: L9's O5, L7's O7 and L13's O8 have none or only weak ones, and in L12 both O12 and O15 flip together. That makes the convention consistent with every other card agreeing. It is the simplest explanation found, not a confirmed one.

Significances test the shapes

LZ's local significances depend on the shape of each recoil spectrum, not its normalisation. They are therefore an independent test of the three forms that change a shape (L6's sign and the O9 terms of L7 and L13), and a null test of the three that only rescale. L12: a cancellation

The O12 and O15 terms of L12 come from one structure. Using the vector identity, valid for v⃗⊥·q⃗ = 0,

[v⃗⊥·(q⃗ × S⃗χ)] (q⃗·S⃗N) = −q⃗2 O12 − mN2 O15(2)

the two enter in the fixed ratio c12/c15 = q⃗2/mN2. ADROIT's card and LZ's printed reduction agree on this, up to an overall sign that no rate can see. Their contributions to xenon's response nearly cancel at exactly that ratio. Detuning it by a fraction ε shows how delicate the cancellation is:

The response is symmetric in ε, so the exact calculation sits at the bottom of the cancellation. A 1% detuning moves the isoscalar limit by up to a factor 2.5. A 5% detuning brings both isospins to 0.96–1.26 of LZ's limits, inside the spread of the other cards. Several conventions could leave such a residue: the nucleon mass in the definition of O15 against the one in mM, or an incomplete interference term between the Φ̃′ and Σ′ responses. With the operator identity exact, ADROIT's weaker limit is the consistent one, but the comparison cannot say which convention in LZ's chain is responsible.

L12: searching for LZ's convention (12 October 2026)

What was done. At the owner's request, we tried to recover LZ's L12 numbers by changing the normalisation conventions until they matched (docs/dm6/lz_l12_conventions.py, results in lz_l12_conventions.json). One rule was fixed before the run: only discrete, physically motivated conventions count, and one is accepted only if it reproduces all thirteen published masses for both isospins at once, within the validation tolerance (a factor 1.3 in rate, so dADROIT/dLZ between 0.877 and 1.140). With a continuously tuned parameter, any one number could be matched, and that would say nothing about LZ's convention. Eighty combinations were tried (sixty distinct: without the interference the sign does not matter):

  • the nucleon mass in O15's definition against the one in the reduction's coefficients: the atomic mass unit throughout (ADROIT), or mp, mn or their mean in O15, which detunes Eq. (2) by 1.5–1.7%;
  • O10's coefficient ×1, ×0, ×½, ×2, or with mM in place of mχ;
  • the O12–O15 interference kept or left out (their rates added);
  • O15's sign as Anand et al., or reversed.

Result. No discrete convention reproduces LZ's L12. The nucleon-mass conventions move the limits most of the way: the isoscalar gap falls from 6–16 to 3–4.5. But none matches even half the entries. With the best one the isovector limits overshoot at high mass (0.55) while the isoscalar ones still fall short at low mass (3.5), which no single convention can fix. Even the tuned detuning, a free parameter, matches at most 21 of 26. The card stays as Anand et al.'s, with the identity exact, and L12 stays unvalidated. ADROIT's L12 limits are the weaker ones, so an exclusion that ADROIT draws from them is conservative.

Why there is an ambiguity at all. Anand et al.'s response functions (their Eq. 38) contain O12 and O15 only through the combination c12 − (q2/mN2) c15 in xenon's two large responses, Φ″ (spin–orbit) and the velocity-dependent part of Σ′. Eq. (2) sets that combination to zero identically. What is left are the small responses: Φ̃′ (proportional to c122), the velocity-suppressed part of Σ″, and O10's q4 term. The rate is a residue, 103–105 times smaller than the rate of the two terms added without their interference. The table shows this directly: dropping the interference, or reversing O15's sign, makes the limits 16–42 times stronger than LZ's, and 33–510 times stronger than the exact calculation. Any choice that breaks Eq. (2) at the percent level then dominates the rate, and several choices that are invisible for every other interaction do so:

  • Eq. (2) relies on v⃗⊥·q⃗ = 0. This holds for the dark matter–nucleus relative velocity, but not for each bound nucleon's, which includes the nucleon's motion inside the nucleus. A calculation that applies the identity before the nuclear matrix elements and one that applies it after differ by exactly such a residue.
  • The nucleon mass appears in three places: the spinor normalisation (through Kμ), the definition of O15, and the scale mM = mN. A code may use different values in each (proton, neutron, average, atomic mass unit).
  • The small Φ̃′ response is the one most sensitive to the nuclear model: WIMpy_NREFT omits it entirely (DM2).

LZ prints its reduction, but not the code-level choices that decide this residue, so the published limits alone cannot fix them. Settling it needs LZ's inputs (the WimPyDD or equivalent configuration for L12). We recommend asking LZ, and meanwhile reading LZ's L12 limits as about 2–16 times stronger than the exact identity gives.

Converting LZ's limits

For an interaction that reduces to one operator, multiply LZ's limit on d by κ to obtain the limit in Anand et al.'s normalisation (with mM = mN; for mM = mv rescale by the card's powers of mM in the usual way):

dAFHup = κ dLZup,   κ(L5) = mN/4mχ,  κ(L8) = mN/2mχ,  κ(L13) = κ(L17) = ½(3)

For L7 no single factor works below about 100 GeV, because of the O9 term. There, and for any model, the safest course is to match the model's own nonrelativistic coefficients to LZ's coefficients in the table above, which state what LZ's d multiplies. Significances and the look-elsewhere correction need no conversion.

Recommendations

For data releases. State each interaction as used, with its nonrelativistic coefficients per unit coupling, the momentum scale mM and the directions of q and v⃗⊥. Here that means stating that L5–L9, L13 and L17 use vector currents and mM = mN. The L12 spectra should be checked against the operator identity of Eq. (2).

For ADROIT (done, 11 October 2026). Anand et al.'s cards stay for physics. The cards used for every comparison with LZ now carry LZ's forms: L5, L7, L8, L13 and L17 as new cards, and L6 with its O3 term reversed, beside L9. Each card's terms match an explicit-spinor reduction of its Lagrangian, and the six together give exactly the five proportional pairs LZ lists. Of the 515 elastic entries, 165 passed the 1.3 tolerance before and 236 pass now, and ADROIT's validated structures rose from 40 to 52 of 61. L12 stays unvalidated until the cancellation is understood.

Limitations

WimPyDD's source was not inspected, since its distribution blocks automated download. The conventions are inferred from LZ's published limits and LZ's 2024 Table I, and each is the simplest one consistent with all the entries. The recomputation is an approximation good to about 30% in d, but the pair test does not depend on it. Isovector couplings are taken as cn = −cp, and the O9 term of L13 is untested. The open cause of the L12 residue is noted above.
